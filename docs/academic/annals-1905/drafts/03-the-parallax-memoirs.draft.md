# DRAFT — ON THE PARALLAX OF BEARINGS AND OF JUDGMENTS

*[First drafts of both memoirs, K and V, autumn 1904, circulated together.
The fair-copy revisions are Paper III. The crossed claims survive here as
first written — including, for honesty's sake, the ones each author
afterwards retracted against their own interest. — Ed.]*

## MEMOIR A (draft) — ON RELAYED BEARINGS. N. Krøger

A view is a question answered within L, from a state no older than F. Two
relays compose: the chart-room's composed warrant is (F_B + L_A, L_A). A
chain of k relays: staleness F₁ + L₂ + … + L_k. Only the origin's
staleness and the relays' latencies compose.

**The standing-world illusion (as first claimed).** *No log that an
observer can keep, and no tally he can cross-check, will distinguish the
world from one that serves his questions standing still — the illusion is
perfect, and cadence has nothing to do with it.*

[Ed. margin: *over-claimed. Vik's letter of 27 September 1904 strikes the
second clause: ask faster than F + L and the world's quickness shows
through the seams — the revised Theorem A2 claims the illusion ONLY above
cadence F + L and claims nothing below. The negative clause is the
theorem's honesty, and the draft did not have it.* — Ed.]

## MEMOIR B (draft) — ON RELAYED JUDGMENTS. A. Vik

A stage of accuracy ρ before a judge of dial r: what is truly within
r − ρ is answered; what is answered is truly within r + ρ. Stages
compound: the effective dial is r + ρ₁ + ρ₂ + … — the sum, not the
greatest, not the product.

**The annulus (as first claimed).** *Every question in the ring between
r − ρ̄ and r + ρ̄ can be flipped by the stages; one play of the stages
flips the whole ring at once.*

[Ed. margin: *the second clause is false and Krøger's letter of 1 October
1904 kills it: one fixed play displaces along one direction and flips a
strip, not a shell — the mercy is per question. The revised Theorem B2
keeps the first clause (which is the theorem) and prints the honesty note
beside it.* — Ed.]

**The moving gauge.** The answers walk; the gauge itself is redrafted.
Budgets: D_a the walked distance, D_m the gauge's perturbation sum.

**Lemma (as first written).** *Across a step, the distance in question
moves by at most* **η_s + step + η_s** *— perturb, take the triangle at the
old gauge, perturb again — so the day's budget is* **D_a + 2·D_m**.

[Ed. margin: *doubled. Krøger's same letter reroutes the backward chain:
perturb ONCE, then take the whole triangle at the new gauge; the budget is
D_a + D_m. The revised Lemma B3 makes the routing explicit and names it
the one-perturbation rule; the revised memoir reprints the doubling, as an
erratum, on the same page as the lemma that prevents it.* — Ed.]

**The drift band.** A held judge differs from the true verdict only where
the margin is within the budget; attained by one key walking to the dial's
edge. **Drift is a stage:** holding against the moved truth is judging the
still truth through a stage of the budget's accuracy.

**The price.** Re-sight every T days at cost c: error at most σρ(T + F);
at fixed error cost cρ/(ε₀ − ρF); at the joint optimum, by the
arithmetic-geometric mean, 2√(cσρ).

*[End of drafts. What the drafts hold that the revisions keep: the
composition laws, the band, the equivalence, the prices. What they hold
that the revisions strike: the cadence-free illusion, the whole-ring flip,
the doubled budget. Three strikes, two of them caught by the other author,
one by its own author against her own interest — the Circle counted this a
good season. — Ed.]*
