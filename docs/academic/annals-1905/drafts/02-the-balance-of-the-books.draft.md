# DRAFT — THE BALANCE OF THE BOOKS

*[First draft, S. Undrum, winter 1903–04, with Grønn's marginal queries in
pencil (transcribed in braces). The slip of the three-legged snap entry is
Fosse's finding, dated in the minute-book 3 May 1904; the printed slip
follows this draft. The revised memoir is Paper II. — Ed.]*

**Sigrun Undrum**

## §1. The creed

Pacioli's rule: every entry sums to nought. We choose it. A posting is
(account, amount); an entry is a set of postings on distinct accounts
carrying a counter-mark; a ledger is the entries applied, with the balances
they leave.

## §2. The cut

A cut is any set of accounts. Φ its total. An entry crosses the cut if it
posts on both sides; else interior. The creed's purchase: the interior may
churn and Φ will not move by one øre.

## §3. Theorem 1 (conservation, interior)

*If no committed entry crosses the cut, Φ is constant.*

Proof by induction over the day's events. Non-applications post nothing.
An application outside the cut touches no cut account. An application
inside the cut belongs to an entry whose cut-side postings exist, and the
entry being interior, all its postings are cut-side; Rule 1 makes the
change nought. ∎ {Grønn, pencil: *"the case 'entry wholly outside, posted
by a cut-side clerk' — can it arise? Rule 2 forbids; say so." — the
revision says so.*}

## §4. Theorem 2 (the staleness claim)

*Under a cut in the mail, the staleness of the far side's mirror against
the near side grows no faster than the in-flight postings: F(t) ≤ F₀ + I(t),
where I(t) is the sum of unmatched cut-side postings.*

{Grønn, pencil, twice underlined: *"F in days, I in kroner. You cannot add
days to kroner. Which meter do you mean? Repair this before anyone outside
the village reads it." — the revision repairs it into two theorems: the
time-staleness grows at rate one day per day from the last delivery, and
the value-deviation EQUALS the mirror in-flight, exactly, at all times.
The draft's conflated inequality is printed here as it stood.* — Ed.}

## §5. Theorem 3 (two clerks)

*Two clerks, the same invoices, any order, any repetition: when all is
served, the balances agree.* Proof: the balance after n invoices is the
opening plus the sum over distinct marks served; a repeated mark adds
nought by Rule 4; the sum does not mention order. ∎

## §6. The shop within a shop

Strike the interior postings; the consolidated balances depend only on
entries touching the exposed accounts. Striking is additive, so interior
entries vanish identically.

## §7. The snap entry

The tally-clockwork compares a guessed g against a sensed s within a
deadband Δ. On exceed, the guess is snapped to the reading and the
correction booked:

    {(G:authority, −1), (T:authority, +1), (G:correction, +|g−s|)}

which plainly balances.

{Grønn, pencil: *"Plainly? Sum the legs." — Ed.: the legs sum to |g−s|, not
nought. Fosse's letter of 3 May 1904 carries the arithmetic; the slip is
printed after this draft; the revised memoir confesses the fault in §6 and
repairs it with the fourth leg, (T:correction-issued, −|g−s|).}

Custody is conserved; the divergence never exceeds Δ at a heartbeat and
Δ + ρ between; the booked corrections grow at most linearly, each snap at
most Δ + ρ and snaps at least ⌈Δ/ρ⌉ beats apart. The debt's interest is
the drift.
