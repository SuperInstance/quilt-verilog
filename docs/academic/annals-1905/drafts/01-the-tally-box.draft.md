# DRAFT — ON THE BOX AND ITS FIVE VERBS

*[First draft, H. Grønn, autumn 1903. Found with the drafts in the trunk,
marked "to be read and corrected" in Skavlan's hand. The Circle's fair copy
of the revised memoir is Paper I; this draft is printed so the corrections
can be seen where they were made. — Ed.]*

**Halvard Grønn**

## §1. What a reckoner is

I went to sea at fifteen and took bearings for forty years. This memoir
writes down what a reckoning is. Let a **Box** mean any honest worker — a
bookkeeper, a lightkeeper, a pilot. Every Box is five organs:

1. its settings;
2. its judgment;
3. its ledger;
4. its clock;
5. its going-on.

## §2. The verbs

Five verbs touch the Box. We have searched for a sixth and found none:

- **BIND** — to set a dial. The tolerance dial is set at the making of the
  Box and needs no minding afterwards.
- **LINK** — to join two Boxes.
- **EFFECT** — to post a balanced entry and move.
- **VIEW** — to ask and be answered.
- **TICK** — to sound the heartbeat.

An account whose work is done is closed and struck. [Ed. margin: *the draft
knew no sixth verb; see Skavlan's letter of 12 November 1903 and the revised
Paper I §2 — FORGET enters the school's grammar by her demand, and with it
the receipt that is always left.*]

## §3. The standing rules

1. **Balance.** Every entry sums to nought. We choose this rule.
2. **One clerk per account.**
3. **One pen, one book.**
4. **The counter-mark.** A mark already in the book is passed over. The
   marks are unique.
5. **Bounded service.**
6. **The tick is never starved.**
7. **Views answer settled books.**

[Ed. margin: *the draft's Rule 4 says only "the marks are unique" — an
assumption with no enforcement; the February 1905 seam incident (Correspond-
ence, Grønn's summing-up) makes the repair, and the revised Paper I points
forward to it. This is our corpus's Counterexample 2 and Theorem 3, the
structural counter-mark. — Ed.]

## §4. The Weigher *(as first annexed by M. Skavlan)*

A judgment is a set of keyed answers matched against a tolerance dial. The
measure of resemblance is a **gauge**: a rule d assigning to every two
things a number, never negative, zero of a thing with itself, the same
either way round, obeying the triangle rule, and **separating**: if two
things are at distance nought they are the same thing.

[Ed. margin: *the draft demands separation (the full metric). Skavlan's
letter of 12 November 1903 kills it with the pink salmon and the humpy —
two names, one fish, distance nought — and the revised Paper I makes the
gauge a pseudometric with "aliases are data" as a theorem. The draft's
§4 had no answer to the alias; the revision is built on it.*]

The dial is set at the making and the Box answers from it. When several
keys answer within the dial the Box may **choose the nearest** and return
it.

[Ed. margin: *"choose the nearest and return it" — the draft permits the
Box to guess. Skavlan's letter strikes this in a sentence: "a weigher that
guesses is a liar with a dial." The revised memoir prints the set and
never chooses: WITHIN, AMBIGUOUS, WITHOUT.*]

## §5. The two-clause lemma

If a stage of error ρ stands before a judge of dial r, what is truly
within r − ρ is answered, and what is answered is truly within r + ρ. This
is obvious and is not proved here.

[Ed. margin: *"obvious and not proved here" — Krøger's letter of 2 December
1903 asks for the proof anyway, and the revised Paper I gives it: the
triangle rule, twice. The school's rule — a claim marked obvious is a claim
marked unexamined — dates from this exchange.*]

## §6. The unit

Measure n quantities in a unit of b smallest steps; the error of the
representable against the true is at most b·√n/2, the corner of the cell.
Compare squared differences against squared deadbands, for the square is
monotone on the non-negative numbers and no root is wanted.

## §7. Claims

The five organs and five verbs exhaust the honest worker; the gauge
separates; the dial is set once; the unit is part of the verdict; the
two-clause lemma holds.

*[End of draft. The reader will note what is absent: the sixth verb, the
alias, the drift, and the proof of the "obvious" lemma. All four arrive by
letter. — Ed.]*
