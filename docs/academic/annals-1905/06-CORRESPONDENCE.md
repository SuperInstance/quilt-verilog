# 06 — THE CORRESPONDENCE

*Letters of the Kaldfjord Circle, 1903–1905, from the trunk in the cannery
loft. Arranged chronologically. The Circle's rule for letters was the same as
its rule for memoirs: misadds are confessed, errata are printed, and nothing
is torn out. The editors have emended only two slips of transcription, in
square brackets, and stripped the fair-copy's duplicate headings; everything
else is as found, seals and all.*

---

**Halvard Grønn to Sigrun Undrum — Kaldfjord, 14 December 1903**

Dear Sigrun,

The Circle has accepted my first memoir, but the tally-box needs its
conservation. I cannot write it; I have not kept a balanced book since the
navy. You alone in this village keep books that balance every night, and
therefore you know what may be posted and what may not. I ask you to take
the second memoir.

The task is this: take Pacioli's axiom — every entry sums to nought — and
prove by induction over the day's events that the total of any set of books
is unchanged by interior activity. Do it by hand, in triplicate, as you do
the season's volumes. Show also that two clerks from the same invoices
converge to the same balances, and that a shop within a shop consolidates
without trace at the boundary. And send me the errata, not only the clean
copy. A proof that hides its corrections is a false proof.

The mail boat brings the ledgers. The bell is our clock.

With respect,
Halvard Grønn

---

**Sigrun Undrum to Halvard Grønn — Kaldfjord, 3 May 1904**

To Halvard Grønn:

I send the tables from the memoir. The March column had a misadd — 4 carried
as 9 — found at the third pass. Corrected in the margin. I trust your eye
for such things. The induction is there, but I would have you proof-read it.
Only you can see the steps as they are.

The books are a mirror. The numbers speak. I write with care. Let me know if
the logic holds. The balance must be exact. As always.

Sigrun Undrum

---

**Nils Krøger to Asta Vik — Kaldfjord, 5 October 1904**

Dear Asta,

I dug out my *Varg II* log last night, the one marked July 1904, and found
our joint notes from the 17th: you and I passed sightings of the mid-strait
herring shoal back and forth from your tower to the cannery's chart nook,
tracking its drift hour by watch. You were the one who pointed out that each
relay's lag added its full latency to the initial sighting's freshness — no
taking the maximum, just straight summation. I'd planned to stake claim to
this additive staleness rule at this week's Circle meeting, but you scrawled
that exact rule in your tally notebook that same night, next to your column
of fathoms and watch offsets.

I'm writing to set the record straight, friend to friend: this result is
first and foremost yours, and I'll bring your original scrap to Thursday's
meeting to pin to our Paper III draft.

Yours,
Nils Krøger, First Mate

---

**Asta Vik to Nils Krøger — Skarvholmen light, 8 October 1904**

Mr. Krøger,

Regarding your claim of 5 October: the additivity is the triangle property;
it has no author, and needs none. Apply the triangle rule twice and the sum
appears; crediting a man for it is like crediting him for the tide.

But the annulus — that inside the blurred ring the verdict is at the stages'
mercy — is mine, dated 11 August, my notebook, witnessed by Marius. Keep
your findings; they are valuable. Yours are the bearings and the standing
world; mine are the ring and the routing.

Not unkind, — A. Vik

---

**Nils Krøger to Asta Vik — aboard *Varg II*, 22 October 1904**

Dear Asta,

Received your 8 October letter just days past, and I laughed out loud — we
have both ceded more ground than we've pressed, which is the only honorable
way a proper scholarly dispute ought to unfold.

I stand by my concession: the additivity bears no single author. You are
within your right to claim the 11 August annulus, witnessed by Marius. Per
the Circle's finding, the relayed-bearing composition and the standing-world
illusion fall to me; the annulus, the one-perturbation rule, the twins, and
the drift band to you. I propose the memoirs be printed under both names,
in the order the work was done — and let the minutes say so.

A sailor's arithmetic, framed at last as a theorem: on the water, a bearing
delayed is a bearing wrong.

Yours,
Nils Krøger

---

**Asta Vik to Nils Krøger — Skarvholmen light, 29 October 1904**

Dear Nils,

Your acceptance of the finding is in good order; joint names, in the order
of work, is right. One correction, flat, for the minutes: the standing-world
illusion is yours alone. I claim no part of it, and the record shall say so.

On the memoirs' real finding, I have thought since the meeting: the twins —
your latencies and my drifts, adding by the same triangle rule at two
organs — are the discovery, and the theorems are its working. Frame the
twins, and the theorems hang themselves.

The light must burn at the appointed hour. That is my cadence rule, and I
hold it in as high regard as you hold yours.

Yours,
A. Vik

---

**Maren Skavlan to Johanne Fosse — Kaldfjord, 20 April 1905**

Dear Johanne,

I believe I have found a use for the residue from the wear-rungs on the
tallies we discussed. By re-crediting the residue into the read-out — the
bank that re-credits as the rungs are read — we can steer the rung-staircase,
centering the read-out on the truth as the fishing seasons go by.

I conjecture that with patience — say, two or three seasons — the bank will
draw the read-out to the true law, closing the two-fold overstatement band
we observed last winter. The separate tallies will still bounce about, but
their centers, I believe, will settle.

Before I write the idea up for the Circle, I ask you to try to break it. You
have a good eye for wishful thinking and shaky assumptions. I would rather
be refuted by a friend than by a season.

Yours,
Maren

---

**Marius Holt to Halvard Grønn — Kaldfjord, 11 June 1905**

Halvard,

Kristiania has refused the memoir. The Academy's words: "a theorem of our
ignorance is not a theorem." I am not surprised; I did not write it for
them.

But I would have your judgment, since theirs is spent. Is a theorem that
says NO, with proof, less true than one that says yes? The floor holds
whatever the Academy thinks of it. A rope is honest; it holds, or it does
not. The bell does not care what the Academy believes either. I rang it at
the hour the letter came.

The Directorate will get the table before the ice. If they refuse it too,
the herring will not, being unable to read.

Marius Holt

---

**Sigrun Undrum to Johanne Fosse — Kaldfjord, 30 June 1905**

Dear Johanne,

The season's books close today. The volumes are closed, sealed, and
triplicate-checked. One slip to confess and record: in the June table a 4
was carried as a 9 in the oil column — found at the third pass, corrected in
the margin. I want it printed in the memoir as found, not smoothed.

When you write what survives the closing of a volume, write what does NOT
survive with the same pen. A book that hides its losses is a false book.

The wear-rail tallies are yours to copy if wanted. The rungs never balance,
and I say so plainly.

S. Undrum

---

**Marius Holt to Johanne Fosse — Kaldfjord, 14 July 1905**

Dear Johanne,

I need the table checked before it goes to the Directorate. You are the only
comptometer in the county I trust.

The work: ρ = 0.748 band-units per night, ε₀ = 0.6. The nightly post gives
ρF = 0.748 > 0.6: the inequality is clear, and the row must stand as
infeasible. Half-nightly, F = 0.5: control cost 3.31 kr per night.
Quarter-nightly: 1.81. The equilibrium sits at F* = 0.401 nights, whole cost
4.99. Run it in triplicate; the ribbon goes to the minute-book with the
memoir.

Two things, plainly. The infeasible row must be flagged, not laundered — the
rate conversion is unregistered and the flag stays. And if the Directorate
refuses the theorem too, the herring will not, being unable to read.

I am wounded but I do not whine.

Marius Holt

---

**Johanne Fosse to Maren Skavlan — Kaldfjord, 9 August 1905**

Dear Maren,

Your residue-bank conjecture is dead, and I am the one who killed it — but I
did not bury it alone. It forced me to find the exact condition, and for
that I thank you.

The deposit table, in quanta, corrected and checked thrice: 18,260; 9,130;
4,565; 2,283; 1,141; 571; 285; 143. At the bottom rung, as built, we were
short by a factor of 18,262 — my first letter said 9,100, and that was my
fault: I lost a factor of two in the octaves and called it found. You will
laugh; I did.

The bank cannot close the band. A non-negative credit only centers the law —
it never tightens. The band stays two-fold wide, but it sits where the truth
is. That is the best that can be had, and it is good.

Come Thursday; I will show you the numbers that killed your beautiful idea,
and we will drink to its ghost.

With affection and exactness,
Johanne Fosse

---

**Maren Skavlan to Johanne Fosse — Kaldfjord, 16 August 1905**

Dear Johanne,

Your letter arrived, and with it the refutation of my conjecture. I concede
fully and without reservation. The credit never dips negative; the upper
ratio only widens, as you so keenly observed. What remains is the centering
— not a tightening of the band, as I had posited, but the deposit holding
the read-out to the middle, rung by rung.

Your correction is noted — an octave slip indeed! But no matter; the
elegance of the refutation shines through. Bounded credit, exact cadence,
honest rate-tracking — these survive and are worth having.

You have given me the schooling I strive to give my own charges — better a
refutation from a friend than a season's silence. I am grateful.

One question remains, and I leave it on the table for the winter: when the
read-out holds the center, is the center where the fishery should stand? Or
is half a band still too wide a berth? I ponder it as I watch the boats sail
out.

Yours with admiration,
Maren

---

**Johanne Fosse to the Circle — Kaldfjord, 12 September 1905** *(to be
pasted into every copy of the fifth memoir)*

To the Circle,

Let the record stand corrected. The noughtth-rung deficit is ~18,262 times,
not ~9,100. The slip was a lost factor of two in the octaves — 2¹⁵ against
2¹⁶ — found on my own third pass and confirmed by Undrum's. Stated once,
plainly.

My creed, without apology: an erratum found is a friend gained; a book that
hides its corrections is a false book; and a bookkeeper who cannot show her
own slips cannot be trusted with yours.

The corrected deposit table stands as printed, with its margin-note: the two
top rungs want 18,262 and 9,131; the table rounds to the ten below; left as
found.

One dry line for the Academy, should they ever read it: we print our
mistakes in the body of the text, not the footnotes, because ours are
load-bearing.

J. Fosse

**Halvard Grønn to the Circle — Kaldfjord, 26 October 1905** *(read at the
season's last meeting)*

To the Whole Circle:

The Fisheries Directorate's October returns are in. Holt's floor stands. The
nightly audit could not hold the band; the half-nightly did, and at the
costs his table gave. Kristiania's refusal stands refuted by herring. Close
that matter.

The five memoirs are one house. The box is grammar; the balance,
conservation; the twins, its blur; the floor, its wall; the fold, its
memory. We do not add rooms. We keep the walls true.

A winter lesson, told as it happened. In February, when the mail was cut,
the cannery and the fleet both minted entries. Two carried the same mark,
both houses numbering from one. At reunion every instrument read "agreed"
while the books were not — the marks collided, and the rule that passes a
known mark made each house blind to the other's entry. The mark must be
BUILT — writer's name and serial — never merely assumed unique. A silent
divergence is worse than a loud one; the loud one can be booked.

What is owed: the sixth verb, FORGET, keeps a receipt. So will we. The
trunk, the minute-book, the errata — all of it kept. Nothing torn out.

H. Grønn

---

**Maren Skavlan to the Circle — Kaldfjord, 2 November 1905**

To the Circle,

Three decades have I taught that a verdict must never guess, and the annals
now say it with proofs. My conjecture, born of an April morning, perished in
August; I count its death among the school's works, for a refutation with
the exact deposit formula attached is worth more than a conjecture left
standing in the dark. To be refuted by a friend is the schooling I give my
own children.

One question I leave open upon the table for the winter: when the read-out
stands in the middle of the band, is the middle where the fishery should
stand? Half a band is still a wide strait to sail by.

And I will say this once, plainly: the schoolroom above the cannery is cold
on Thursdays, and I would not have spent these three winters anywhere else.

Maren

---

**Nils Krøger to Asta Vik — Kaldfjord, 5 November 1905**

Dear Asta,

The season's final mail run is tied up for winter, leaving me a quiet hour
by the port lantern. Our parallax memoirs stand printed side by side under
both names, in the order of the work.

On the last crossing I worked the chain arithmetic again by the ship's lamp:
four stations, two hours apiece, three hours stale at the origin — eleven
hours stale in the chart-room, and the log honest about every hour of it. I
carry from the work not the theorem but the habit: a late bearing is not a
slow bearing, it is a wrong bearing — and I have passed that one sentence to
every man in the fleet.

The dry joke, for the record: the twins — your drift and my delay — wed
between these pages. The Academy will spurn the union; the strait will
conduct it anyway.

The notebook of 11 August is the school's property now, but I beg you hold
it close; the light needs its arithmetic.

Yours always,
Nils Krøger

---

**Asta Vik to Nils Krøger — Skarvholmen light, 9 November 1905**

Dear Nils,

The notebook page is copied and filed with the minute-book; the original
stays on the rock, where the light's arithmetic is done daily and the
archive's never is.

One correction, flat, for the record: the first draft of the one-perturbation
lemma carried the doubled budget [D_a + 2·D_m] throughout, and you caught it
at first reading. The minutes credit you for the catch, under my lemma's
name — errors have authors too.

On your wedding: twins do not marry. They take turns.

Yours faithfully,
Asta Vik

---

**Sigrun Undrum to Maren Skavlan — Kaldfjord, 18 November 1905** *(the last
letter in the trunk)*

Maren,

The winter's volumes are shut, and the loft key lies with the notary. As to
your open question — whether the middle of the band is where the fishery
should stand — my answer, as a bookkeeper and not a mathematician, is this:
a bookkeeper stands where the receipts are, and the receipts live at the
edges, not the middle. I suspect your question is really how much blur a
fisherman can be paid to carry, and that is a question about men, not rungs.

The books balance. The schoolroom is let for the winter. The Circle meets
again when the strait opens.

S. Undrum

---

*Thus the trunk. The editors count eighteen letters, one refuted conjecture,
one erratum published by its own author, one refusal by an Academy
overturned by a fishery, one priority dispute conducted by mutual surrender,
and one question left open on the table for a winter that lasted a hundred
and twenty years.*
